## 2. Answers to Phase 7 Viva Explanation Questions

### Question 1: Why is dual reporting for the baseline (`Design P` vs `Design P_bus`) mandatory under the fairness protocol?

**Answer:**
Comparing a specialized fixed-weight design against a programmable baseline requires careful accounting of storage overhead:
1. **The Core Question:** Does constant-weight folding reduce the arithmetic datapath itself, or does the area advantage come purely from removing weight flip-flops?
2. **`Design P_bus` (Logic-Only Datapath):** By providing weights as primary input wires without on-chip register storage, `Design P_bus` contains only the combinational multipliers, addition tree, and registered output accumulators. Comparing Design F against Design P_bus isolates the pure arithmetic datapath simplification (constant shift-and-add trees vs general $8 \times 8$ multiplier arrays). The results prove that even ignoring weight storage, Design F achieves a **3.14× to 3.79× reduction in cell count** over Design P_bus.
3. **`Design P` (Storage-Inclusive Baseline):** Real standalone accelerators that do not stream weights from off-chip pads every cycle must store weights locally. In standard cells, storing an $N \times K$ weight array in flip-flops requires $8 \cdot N \cdot K$ registers. Comparing Design F against Design P reflects total on-chip standard-cell area, showing a **3.18× to 3.85× overall cell reduction** and a **17.7× reduction in flip-flops**.
4. **Honest Accounting:** In enterprise ASICs, programmable weights are often held in dense on-chip SRAM macros rather than flip-flops. By reporting both `P_bus` and `P`, our thesis remains fully transparent, robust to reviewer scrutiny, and prevents any accusations of unfair comparison.

### Question 2: Why are real LLM weights ~23% smaller to synthesize than uniform random INT8 numbers?

**Answer:**
In Experiment E2, an identical $8 \times 16$ tile synthesized with real weights required **22,764 cells**, whereas uniform random INT8 numbers across 5 seeds required an average of **28,005 cells** (+23.0% more logic).

This occurs due to the mathematical distribution of weights in trained transformer networks:
1. **Zero-Centered Gaussian Distribution:** Trained neural network weights are typically normal or Student-$t$ distributed with mean $\mu \approx 0$ and standard deviation $\sigma \ll 127$. Consequently, when quantized to INT8 $[-127, 127]$, the majority of coefficients have small absolute magnitudes ($|w| \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, \dots, 20\}$) and low Hamming weights (fewer active binary bits).
2. **Constant Multiplication Simplification:** Multiplying an input $x$ by a constant $w$ is implemented by Yosys as shift-and-add operations:
   - For $w = 2$: $x \ll 1$ (0 adders, pure wire rewiring).
   - For $w = 3$: $(x \ll 1) + x$ (1 adder).
   - For uniform random numbers uniformly distributed across $[-127, 127]$, coefficients frequently have 4 to 6 active bits (e.g. $w = 119 = 01110111_2$), requiring 3 to 4 cascaded additions per product.
3. **Empirical Proof:** This hypothesis was directly proven by the `magnitude_matched` control in E2. When random signs and positions were applied to the real weight magnitude histogram, the cell count was $23,254$ cells (within 2% of real weights), whereas uniform random numbers required $28,006$ cells. Thus, magnitude distribution is the primary driver of datapath area reduction.

### Question 3: How does constant-weight specialization shorten the combinational critical path?

**Answer:**
In Experiment E3, measuring longest topological path (`ltp`) depth in generic gates revealed that Design F reduces critical path delay by **20% to 32%** compared to Design P (critical path ratio $0.68 - 0.74$):

1. **Multiplier Array Depth vs Shift-Add Depth:**
   - In Design P, an $8 \times 8$ signed multiplier requires generating 64 partial products, followed by a Wallace or Dadda reduction tree of full/half adders, and a final carry-propagate adder, creating 40+ gates of combinational depth before the row accumulation tree even begins.
   - In Design F, constant multiplication generates at most 1 to 2 addition stages (or simple wire shifts).
2. **Early Pruning & Range Bounding:**
   - Because weights are known constants, the upper bits of partial products and accumulators are statically bounded. Yosys logic optimization passes (`opt`, `opt_expr`, `opt_clean`) prune unreachable carry paths and propagate constant zeros and signs forward.
3. **Shallower Row Accumulation:**
   - Zero coefficients contribute 0 terms to the row adder tree, decreasing the effective number of operands and pruning adder tree branches.

### Question 4: How was the full-layer extrapolation model validated, and why is the estimation error low?

**Answer:**
Full transformer linear layers in SmolLM2-135M contain up to $1536 \times 576$ matrices ($884,736$ coefficients), which cannot be synthesized as a single unrolled flat module in Yosys without hitting memory and timeout limits. We therefore modeled full-layer cost by scaling per-tile benchmarks:

1. **Model Formulation:**
   $$\text{Area}_{\text{extrap}} = N_{\text{tiles}} \times \text{Area}_{\text{tile}} + \text{Area}_{\text{reduction\_tree}}$$
   where inter-tile adder reduction trees sum partial tile accumulations across column tiles:
   $$\text{Area}_{\text{reduction\_tree}} = N \times (N_{\text{col\_tiles}} - 1) \times \text{Cost}_{\text{adder}}$$
2. **Empirical Direct Validation:**
   - Rather than relying on theoretical assumptions, we directly synthesized a multi-tile core ($16 \times 32$ matrix with $4$ tiles of $8 \times 16$) with full top-level registered wrappers and adder reduction trees.
   - Measured actual multi-tile area:
     * Design F: $83,551$ cells actual vs $92,592$ extrapolated ($\epsilon = 10.82\%$ error, conservative overestimate).
     * Design P: $298,733$ cells actual vs $302,832$ extrapolated ($\epsilon = 1.37\%$ error).
     * Design P_bus: $293,737$ cells actual vs $297,976$ extrapolated ($\epsilon = 1.44\%$ error).
3. **Why the Error is Low:**
   - The tile interface boundary is strictly registered and decoupled.
   - For Design F, the top-level Yosys `opt` pass performs modest cross-tile optimization (e.g. sharing identical sub-expressions across tiles), which slightly lowers the actual area below the linear sum, making our linear extrapolation conservative.

### Question 5: What are the fundamental limitations of pre-layout logic synthesis metrics?

**Answer:**
In accordance with Rule B2 and academic rigor, all synthesis conclusions must be qualified with standard pre-layout limitations:

1. **Pre-Layout vs Post-Layout:**
   - Generic gate synthesis estimates logic gate count (AND, OR, XOR, MUX, DFF). It does not include standard cell placement, clock tree synthesis (CTS), scan chain insertion, power distribution networks, or physical routing congestion.
   - In physical layout, dense constant wiring networks could introduce routing congestion, whereas regular systolic arrays in programmable accelerators have predictable abutted routing.
2. **Topological Delay vs STA:**
   - `ltp` measures the number of logic gates along the longest topological path. It does not measure wire RC delay, pin capacitance, or setup/hold slack across process-voltage-temperature (PVT) operating corners.
3. **Memory Macro Representation:**
   - Programmable baselines in production chips utilize high-density SRAM macros rather than standard-cell D flip-flops. However, our inclusion of `Design P_bus` cleanly sidesteps this limitation by evaluating the arithmetic datapath strictly without storage.
4. **Scope Boundaries:**
   - Part I evaluates inference on fixed dense linear layers. It does not synthesize non-linear activation functions (SiLU), layer normalization (RMSNorm), attention softmax, or KV cache storage.
